# HIPAA Enforcement Rule Summary

**Regulation:** 45 CFR Part 160, Subparts C, D, E  
**Effective Date:** February 16, 2006  
**Last Updated:** January 2013 (Omnibus Rule)  
**Category:** regulations  
**Critical:** true

---

## Overview

The HIPAA Enforcement Rule establishes procedures for investigations, hearings, and imposition of civil money penalties for violations of the HIPAA Administrative Simplification Rules (Privacy, Security, Breach Notification, and Transactions).

The Rule is enforced by the Office for Civil Rights (OCR) within the Department of Health and Human Services (HHS).

---

## Enforcement Authority

### Office for Civil Rights (OCR)

**Responsibilities:**
- Investigate complaints
- Conduct compliance reviews
- Perform audits
- Impose civil money penalties
- Negotiate resolution agreements

**Website:** https://www.hhs.gov/ocr

**Complaint Portal:** https://ocrportal.hhs.gov/ocr/smartscreen/main.jsf

---

## Violation Categories

### Tier 1: Unknowing

**Definition:** Individual did not know and could not have known of the violation

**Penalty Range:** $100 - $50,000 per violation

**Example:** Employee accidentally emails PHI to wrong recipient despite training and policies

---

### Tier 2: Reasonable Cause

**Definition:** Violation due to reasonable cause, not willful neglect

**Penalty Range:** $1,000 - $50,000 per violation

**Example:** System vulnerability exploited despite reasonable security measures

---

### Tier 3: Willful Neglect (Corrected)

**Definition:** Conscious, intentional failure or reckless indifference, but corrected within 30 days

**Penalty Range:** $10,000 - $50,000 per violation

**Example:** No risk analysis conducted, but completed within 30 days of notification

---

### Tier 4: Willful Neglect (Not Corrected)

**Definition:** Conscious, intentional failure or reckless indifference, NOT corrected within 30 days

**Penalty Range:** $50,000 per violation (mandatory minimum)

**Example:** No encryption, no risk analysis, not corrected after OCR notification

**Annual Cap:** $1.5 million per violation category

---

## Investigation Process

### 1. Complaint Filed

**Who Can File:**
- Individuals (patients)
- Employees
- Business associates
- Anonymous tips

**Timeline:** Within **180 days** of incident (or when discovered)

**Extension:** OCR may extend for good cause

**Information Required:**
- Name and contact of complainant
- Name of covered entity
- Description of violation
- Date of violation

---

### 2. Intake and Review

**OCR Reviews:**
- Jurisdiction (is it HIPAA-covered?)
- Timeliness (within 180 days?)
- Actionable (specific violation alleged?)

**Outcomes:**
- **Accepted** - Investigation proceeds
- **Rejected** - No jurisdiction or untimely
- **Referred** - To state agency or other authority

---

### 3. Investigation

**OCR May:**
- Request documents
- Interview witnesses
- Conduct on-site visits
- Review policies and procedures
- Examine audit logs
- Test systems

**Entity Must:**
- Cooperate fully
- Provide requested information
- Allow access to facilities
- Respond within specified timeframes

**Timeline:** Typically 6-12 months

---

### 4. Resolution

#### Option A: No Violation Found

**Outcome:** Case closed, no penalty

**Reason:** Insufficient evidence or no violation occurred

---

#### Option B: Voluntary Compliance

**Outcome:** Corrective Action Plan (CAP)

**Requirements:**
- Implement specific corrective actions
- Provide documentation
- Training
- Policy updates
- Monitoring period (1-3 years)

**No Penalty** if voluntarily complied

---

#### Option C: Resolution Agreement

**Outcome:** Settlement with OCR

**Includes:**
- **Monetary Settlement** - Negotiated amount
- **Corrective Action Plan** - Specific requirements
- **Monitoring** - OCR oversight (1-3 years)
- **Reporting** - Regular compliance reports

**Publicly Announced** - Press release and website posting

---

#### Option D: Civil Money Penalty

**Outcome:** Formal penalty imposed

**Process:**
- OCR issues Notice of Proposed Determination
- Entity has 90 days to request hearing
- Administrative Law Judge (ALJ) hearing
- Final determination

**Publicly Announced**

---

## Factors Affecting Penalties

### Aggravating Factors (Increase Penalty)

- Prior violations
- Financial condition (can afford higher penalty)
- Failure to cooperate
- Harm to individuals
- Number of individuals affected
- Duration of violation
- Willful neglect

### Mitigating Factors (Decrease Penalty)

- Good faith effort to comply
- Limited financial resources
- Prompt corrective action
- No prior violations
- Cooperation with investigation
- No harm to individuals
- Small number affected

---

## Criminal Penalties

**Enforced by:** Department of Justice (DOJ)

### Tier 1: Wrongful Disclosure

**Penalty:** Up to $50,000 fine and 1 year in prison

**Example:** Employee accesses celebrity's medical record out of curiosity

---

### Tier 2: False Pretenses

**Penalty:** Up to $100,000 fine and 5 years in prison

**Example:** Person poses as patient to obtain medical records

---

### Tier 3: Intent to Sell/Transfer/Use for Commercial Advantage

**Penalty:** Up to $250,000 fine and 10 years in prison

**Example:** Employee sells patient database to marketing company

## Notable Enforcement Actions

### Largest Settlements (2013-2025)

1. **Anthem Inc.** - $16 million (2018)
   - Breach affecting 79 million individuals
   - Lack of risk analysis
   - Insufficient security measures

2. **Premera Blue Cross** - $6.85 million (2019)
   - Breach affecting 10.4 million individuals
   - No risk analysis
   - Inadequate network security

3. **University of Texas MD Anderson Cancer Center** - $4.3 million (2018)
   - Three separate breaches
   - Unencrypted devices
   - Lack of risk analysis

4. **Advocate Health Care** - $5.55 million (2016)
   - Breach affecting 4 million individuals
   - Unencrypted devices stolen

5. **New York-Presbyterian Hospital** - $4.8 million (2014)
   - Breach affecting 6,800 individuals
   - Failure to implement security measures

### Common Violations

1. **Lack of Risk Analysis** (most common)
2. **No Encryption** (especially mobile devices)
3. **Insufficient Access Controls**
4. **No Business Associate Agreements**
5. **Failure to Provide Access** (patient rights)
6. **No Audit Controls**
7. **Inadequate Training**
8. **Improper Disposal**

## Complaint Process

### Filing a Complaint

**Portal:** https://ocrportal.hhs.gov/ocr/smartscreen/main.jsf

**Alternative:** Paper form (OCR Complaint Form)

**Information Needed:**
1. Your name and contact (can be anonymous)
2. Name of covered entity
3. Description of what happened
4. When it happened
5. How you were harmed

### Timeline

- **180 days** from incident (or discovery)
- Extensions possible for good cause

### What Happens

1. **Acknowledgment** - OCR confirms receipt
2. **Review** - Determines jurisdiction
3. **Investigation** - If accepted
4. **Resolution** - Various outcomes
5. **Notification** - You're notified of outcome

### Confidentiality

- Complainant identity protected (unless consent given)
- Investigation details confidential
- Resolution may be public (if settlement/penalty)

---

## Defending Against Complaints

### Best Practices

1. **Cooperate Fully**
   - Respond promptly
   - Provide requested documents
   - Be truthful and complete

2. **Document Everything**
   - Policies and procedures
   - Training records
   - Risk assessments
   - Incident response
   - Corrective actions

3. **Show Good Faith**
   - Demonstrate compliance efforts
   - Highlight corrective actions
   - Show no harm occurred (if applicable)

4. **Legal Counsel**
   - Consider HIPAA attorney
   - Especially for large breaches
   - Or if penalties proposed

5. **Corrective Action**
   - Implement immediately
   - Document thoroughly
   - Prevent recurrence

## State Enforcement

### State Attorneys General

**Authority:** Can enforce HIPAA (since 2009)

**Process:**
- Investigate violations
- File civil actions
- Obtain damages for residents
- Seek injunctions

**Examples:**
- Connecticut AG - $250,000 from Health Net
- Vermont AG - $150,000 from Rite Aid

### State Breach Notification Laws

Many states have additional breach notification laws:
- May have shorter timelines
- May cover non-HIPAA entities
- May have different thresholds
- May require state AG notification

**Must comply with BOTH federal and state laws**

## Avoiding Enforcement Actions

### Top 10 Tips

1. **Conduct Annual Risk Analysis** - Most common violation
2. **Encrypt All ePHI** - Safe harbor protection
3. **Train Workforce** - Annual training required
4. **Implement BAAs** - All vendors must sign
5. **Monitor Audit Logs** - Detect unauthorized access
6. **Respond to Patient Requests** - Within 30 days
7. **Have Incident Response Plan** - Test annually
8. **Document Everything** - Policies, training, incidents
9. **Update Regularly** - Policies, software, training
10. **Cooperate with OCR** - If investigation occurs
