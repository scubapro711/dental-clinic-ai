# HIPAA PHI Handling FAQs

This document provides answers to frequently asked questions about handling Protected Health Information (PHI) under HIPAA.

## What is Considered PHI?

Protected Health Information (PHI) is any individually identifiable health information that is transmitted or maintained in any form or medium by a covered entity or its business associate. This includes demographic information, medical histories, test results, insurance information, and other data that can be used to identify a patient. [1]

## What are the Minimum Necessary Standards for PHI Access?

The Minimum Necessary Standard requires covered entities to make reasonable efforts to limit the use or disclosure of, and requests for, PHI to the minimum necessary to accomplish the intended purpose. This means that workforce members should only have access to the PHI they need to perform their job duties.

## How Should PHI be Disposed Of?

When PHI is no longer needed, it must be disposed of in a way that it cannot be reconstructed. This includes shredding or otherwise destroying paper records and permanently deleting electronic records.

## Can PHI be Used for Marketing Purposes?

HIPAA requires an individual's written authorization before their PHI can be used for marketing purposes. There are a few exceptions to this rule, such as communications about a drug or biologic that the individual is currently being prescribed. [2]
