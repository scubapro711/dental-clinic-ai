# HIPAA Business Associate Agreement (BAA) FAQs

This document provides answers to frequently asked questions about Business Associate Agreements (BAAs) under HIPAA.

## What is a Business Associate?

A Business Associate is a person or entity that performs certain functions or activities that involve the use or disclosure of Protected Health Information (PHI) on behalf of, or provides services to, a covered entity. [1]

## When is a BAA Required?

A covered entity must have a written contract, a Business Associate Agreement (BAA), with a Business Associate before any PHI can be shared. This agreement is required for all vendors and subcontractors who may come into contact with PHI.

## What are the Key Components of a BAA?

A BAA must include the following key components:

| Component | Description |
|---|---|
| **Permitted Uses and Disclosures of PHI** | The agreement must specify the permitted and required uses and disclosures of PHI by the business associate. |
| **Safeguards** | The business associate must agree to implement appropriate safeguards to prevent the use or disclosure of PHI other than as provided for by the contract. |
| **Reporting of Unauthorized Disclosures** | The business associate must agree to report to the covered entity any use or disclosure of PHI not provided for by the contract, including breaches of unsecured PHI. |
| **Subcontractor Compliance** | The business associate must ensure that any subcontractors that create, receive, maintain, or transmit PHI on behalf of the business associate agree to the same restrictions and conditions that apply to the business associate. |
| **Access to PHI** | The business associate must make PHI available to the covered entity as necessary to allow the covered entity to meet its obligations to provide individuals with access to their PHI. |
| **Termination of the Agreement** | The agreement must authorize the termination of the contract by the covered entity if the business associate violates a material term of the contract. |
