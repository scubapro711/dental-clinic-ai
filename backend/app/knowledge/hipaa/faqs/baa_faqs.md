### 1. What is a Business Associate Agreement (BAA)?
A Business Associate Agreement (BAA) is a written contract between a HIPAA-covered entity and a business associate. It is required by HIPAA to ensure that the business associate will appropriately safeguard protected health information (PHI).

### 2. Who is considered a business associate?
A business associate is a person or entity that performs certain functions or activities that involve the use or disclosure of PHI on behalf of, or provides services to, a covered entity. Examples include a third-party administrator that assists a health plan with claims processing, or a consultant that performs utilization reviews for a hospital.

### 3. Is a BAA required for all vendors?
No, a BAA is not required for all vendors. It is only required for vendors who are considered business associates. For example, a BAA is not required for a janitorial service, as they do not have access to PHI.

### 4. What are the key elements of a BAA?
A BAA must establish the permitted and required uses and disclosures of PHI by the business associate. It must also require the business associate to implement appropriate safeguards to prevent unauthorized use or disclosure of PHI.

### 5. What happens if a BAA is not in place?
If a covered entity fails to enter into a BAA with a business associate, both parties may be subject to civil and criminal penalties under HIPAA.

### 6. Can a business associate be held liable for a HIPAA violation?
Yes, business associates are directly liable for their own non-compliance with HIPAA, just as covered entities are.

### 7. Can a BAA be in electronic form?
Yes, a BAA can be in electronic form and can be electronically signed.

### 8. Does a BAA need to be updated?
A BAA should be reviewed and updated as needed to reflect any changes in the relationship between the covered entity and the business associate, or any changes in the HIPAA regulations.

### 9. What is a "conduit" and are they considered a business associate?
A conduit is an entity that only transmits PHI and does not have access to it, such as the US Postal Service. Conduits are not considered business associates and do not require a BAA.

### 10. Are cloud service providers (CSPs) considered business associates?
Yes, CSPs that create, receive, maintain, or transmit ePHI on behalf of a covered entity or another business associate are considered business associates, even if they only store encrypted ePHI and do not have a decryption key.

### 11. What if a CSP experiences a security incident?
The CSP must report the security incident to the covered entity or business associate.

### 12. Can a covered entity use a CSP that stores ePHI outside of the United States?
Yes, the HIPAA Rules do not prohibit the use of a CSP that stores ePHI on servers outside of the United States. However, the covered entity must ensure that the CSP has appropriate safeguards in place to protect the ePHI.

### 13. Do software vendors need a BAA?
It depends. If the software vendor has access to PHI, then a BAA is required. For example, if a software vendor provides a cloud-based practice management system, they would be a business associate. However, if the software is installed on the covered entity's own servers and the vendor does not have access to the data, a BAA may not be required.

### 14. Are physicians with hospital privileges considered business associates of the hospital?
No, physicians with hospital privileges are not considered business associates of the hospital. They are part of the hospital's workforce for the services they provide at the hospital.

### 15. Is a reinsurer a business associate of a health plan?
Yes, a reinsurer that receives PHI from a health plan is a business associate of the health plan.

### 16. What is the difference between a business associate and a subcontractor?
A subcontractor is a person or entity to whom a business associate delegates a function, activity, or service, other than as a member of its workforce. If a business associate hires a subcontractor that will have access to PHI, the business associate must enter into a BAA with the subcontractor.

### 17. Can a business associate use or disclose PHI for their own purposes?
No, a business associate may only use or disclose PHI as permitted by the BAA and as required by law.

### 18. What are the responsibilities of a business associate in the event of a breach?
A business associate must notify the covered entity of a breach of unsecured PHI without unreasonable delay and in no case later than 60 calendar days after discovery of the breach.

### 19. Can a business associate de-identify PHI?
Yes, a business associate may de-identify PHI, but only if the BAA permits them to do so.

### 20. What is the relationship between a BAA and a data use agreement (DUA)?
A DUA is an agreement required by the Privacy Rule for the use or disclosure of a limited data set. A limited data set is PHI that excludes certain direct identifiers. If a business associate is creating or receiving a limited data set, a DUA is required in addition to a BAA.
