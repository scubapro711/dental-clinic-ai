# HIPAA Patient Rights FAQ

This document answers common questions about patient rights under the HIPAA Privacy Rule.

**1. What are my basic rights over my health information?**

The HIPAA Privacy Rule gives you the right to:

*   Ask to see and get a copy of your health records.
*   Have corrections added to your health information.
*   Receive a notice that tells you how your health information may be used and shared.
*   Decide if you want to give your permission before your health information can be used or shared for certain purposes, such as for marketing.
*   Get a report on when and why your health information was shared for certain purposes.

**2. How can I access my medical records?**

You can ask your provider or health plan for a copy of your medical records. They must provide you with a copy within 30 days. They may charge a reasonable, cost-based fee for the copies.

**3. What if I find a mistake in my medical records?**

You have the right to ask for a correction to be added to your health information. Your provider or health plan must respond to your request within 60 days.

**4. Who can look at and receive my health information?**

Your health information can be used and shared for your treatment, payment, and health care operations. It can also be shared with your family, friends, or others you identify as involved in your health care, unless you object.

**5. What if I believe my rights are being violated?**

If you believe your rights are being denied or your health information isn’t being protected, you can:

*   File a complaint with your provider or health insurer.
*   File a complaint with the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS).

*Source: [HHS.gov - Your Rights Under HIPAA](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-individuals/guidance-materials-for-consumers/index.html)*
