### 1. What is a breach of unsecured PHI?
A breach is an impermissible use or disclosure of protected health information (PHI) that compromises the security or privacy of the PHI.

### 2. What is unsecured PHI?
Unsecured PHI is PHI that has not been rendered unusable, unreadable, or indecipherable to unauthorized individuals through the use of a technology or methodology specified by the Secretary of HHS, such as encryption.

### 3. What is the Breach Notification Rule?
The Breach Notification Rule requires covered entities and their business associates to provide notification following a breach of unsecured PHI.

### 4. Who must be notified in the event of a breach?
In the event of a breach, covered entities must notify affected individuals, the Secretary of HHS, and, in some cases, the media.

### 5. When must individuals be notified of a breach?
Individuals must be notified without unreasonable delay and in no case later than 60 days following the discovery of a breach.

### 6. How must individuals be notified of a breach?
Individuals must be notified in writing by first-class mail, or by email if the individual has agreed to receive electronic notice. If a covered entity has insufficient contact information for 10 or more individuals, it must provide substitute notice, such as by posting the notice on its website.

### 7. What information must be included in the notification to individuals?
The notification must include a brief description of the breach, a description of the types of information involved, the steps individuals should take to protect themselves, a brief description of what the covered entity is doing to investigate the breach and mitigate harm, and contact information for the covered entity.

### 8. When must the Secretary of HHS be notified of a breach?
If a breach affects 500 or more individuals, the Secretary must be notified without unreasonable delay and in no case later than 60 days following the discovery of a breach. If a breach affects fewer than 500 individuals, the Secretary may be notified on an annual basis.

### 9. When must the media be notified of a breach?
If a breach affects more than 500 residents of a state or jurisdiction, the covered entity must provide notice to prominent media outlets serving the state or jurisdiction.

### 10. What are the responsibilities of a business associate in the event of a breach?
A business associate must notify the covered entity of a breach without unreasonable delay and in no case later than 60 days following the discovery of the breach.

### 11. What is the burden of proof in a breach investigation?
The covered entity or business associate has the burden of proof to demonstrate that all required notifications have been provided or that a use or disclosure of unsecured PHI did not constitute a breach.

### 12. What is a risk assessment?
A risk assessment is a process to determine the probability that PHI has been compromised. It must consider the nature and extent of the PHI involved, the unauthorized person who used the PHI or to whom the disclosure was made, whether the PHI was actually acquired or viewed, and the extent to which the risk to the PHI has been mitigated.

### 13. Are there any exceptions to the definition of a breach?
Yes, there are three exceptions: (1) unintentional acquisition, access, or use of PHI by a workforce member in good faith; (2) inadvertent disclosure of PHI to another authorized person at the same covered entity; and (3) if the covered entity has a good faith belief that the unauthorized person to whom the disclosure was made would not have been able to retain the information.

### 14. What are the penalties for failing to comply with the Breach Notification Rule?
The penalties for failing to comply with the Breach Notification Rule can include civil monetary penalties and, in some cases, criminal penalties.

### 15. What should a covered entity do to prepare for a breach?
A covered entity should have written policies and procedures in place for breach notification, train employees on these policies and procedures, and have a plan to investigate and mitigate any potential breaches.

### 16. What is the role of the FTC in breach notification?
The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) enforces similar breach notification provisions for vendors of personal health records and their third-party service providers.

### 17. How can a breach be prevented?
A breach can be prevented by implementing strong security measures, such as encryption, and by training employees on how to handle PHI properly.

### 18. What should an individual do if they believe their PHI has been breached?
An individual should contact the covered entity to inquire about the breach and may also file a complaint with the Secretary of HHS.

### 19. Can a covered entity delegate the responsibility of providing individual notices to a business associate?
Yes, a covered entity may delegate the responsibility of providing individual notices to a business associate.

### 20. What are the administrative requirements for breach notification?
Covered entities must have written policies and procedures for breach notification, train employees on these policies and procedures, and apply appropriate sanctions against workforce members who do not comply.
