### 1. What are my fundamental rights under HIPAA?
Under HIPAA, you have the right to:
- Access, inspect, and get a copy of your health records.
- Request corrections to your health information.
- Receive a notice of privacy practices that explains how your health information is used and shared.
- Give your permission (authorization) before your health information is used for marketing or other specific purposes.
- Request restrictions on how your health information is used or disclosed.
- Receive a report on when and why your health information was shared for certain purposes (an accounting of disclosures).
- File a complaint if you believe your rights have been violated.

### 2. How do I get a copy of my medical records?
You can ask your provider or health insurer for a copy of your medical records. They must provide you with a copy within 30 days of your request.

### 3. Can my provider charge me for a copy of my records?
Yes, your provider can charge a reasonable, cost-based fee for the copies.

### 4. What if I find a mistake in my medical records?
You have the right to request that your provider correct any mistakes in your medical records. Your provider must respond to your request within 60 days.

### 5. Can my health information be shared without my permission?
Your health information can be shared without your permission for purposes of treatment, payment, and health care operations. For example, your primary care physician can share your information with a specialist they are referring you to.

### 6. Can my health information be given to my employer?
Generally, your provider cannot give your health information to your employer without your authorization.

### 7. Can my health information be used for marketing?
Your health information cannot be used for marketing purposes without your written authorization.

### 8. Can I find out who my health information has been shared with?
Yes, you have the right to receive an accounting of disclosures, which is a list of certain disclosures of your health information made by your provider or health plan.

### 9. How do I file a complaint if I think my rights have been violated?
You can file a complaint with your provider or health insurer, or you can file a complaint with the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS).

### 10. Can my family members access my health information?
Your provider may share your health information with a family member or other person involved in your care if you do not object.

### 11. What is a personal representative?
A personal representative is a person who has legal authority to make health care decisions on your behalf, such as a parent of a minor child or a legal guardian.

### 12. Do I have the right to restrict disclosures to a health plan?
You have the right to restrict certain disclosures of PHI to a health plan if you pay for a service or health care item out-of-pocket in full.

### 13. What is a notice of privacy practices?
A notice of privacy practices is a document that explains how a covered entity may use and disclose your PHI, your rights with respect to your PHI, and the covered entity’s legal duties with respect to your PHI.

### 14. How often should I receive a notice of privacy practices?
You should receive a notice of privacy practices from your provider or health plan at the time of your first service delivery.

### 15. Can I request confidential communications?
Yes, you can request that your provider or health plan communicate with you in a certain way or at a certain location, such as by sending mail to a post office box instead of your home address.

### 16. What are my rights regarding my mental health information?
Your mental health information is protected under HIPAA in the same way as your other health information. However, there are some special protections for psychotherapy notes.

### 17. What are psychotherapy notes?
Psychotherapy notes are notes recorded by a mental health professional that document or analyze the contents of a conversation during a private counseling session or a group, joint, or family counseling session.

### 18. Are psychotherapy notes part of my medical record?
No, psychotherapy notes are kept separate from the rest of your medical record.

### 19. Can my psychotherapy notes be disclosed without my authorization?
Generally, psychotherapy notes cannot be disclosed without your written authorization.

### 20. What are my rights if I am a minor?
If you are a minor, your parents are generally your personal representatives and can exercise your HIPAA rights on your behalf. However, there are some exceptions, such as when a minor is legally able to consent to their own medical treatment.
