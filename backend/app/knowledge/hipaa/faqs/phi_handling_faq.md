# HIPAA Protected Health Information (PHI) Handling FAQ

This document answers common questions about handling Protected Health Information (PHI) under HIPAA.

**1. What is considered Protected Health Information (PHI)?**

PHI is any individually identifiable health information that is transmitted or maintained in any form or medium (electronic, paper, or oral) by a covered entity or its business associate. This includes demographic data, medical histories, test results, insurance information, and other information that could be used to identify a patient.

**2. What are the basic principles of handling PHI?**

The HIPAA Privacy Rule establishes the principle of "minimum necessary," which means that covered entities and business associates should only use, disclose, and request the minimum amount of PHI needed to accomplish the intended purpose.

**3. What are some best practices for safeguarding PHI?**

*   **Access Controls:** Implement technical policies and procedures to ensure that only authorized individuals have access to electronic PHI (ePHI).
*   **Encryption:** Encrypt ePHI at rest and in transit to protect it from unauthorized access.
*   **Employee Training:** Train all employees with access to PHI on HIPAA policies and procedures.
*   **Secure Disposal:** Properly dispose of PHI in all forms, including shredding paper records and securely wiping electronic media.
*   **Auditing:** Regularly audit access to ePHI to detect and respond to security incidents.

**4. Can PHI be used for marketing purposes?**

A covered entity must obtain an individual's written authorization before using or disclosing their PHI for marketing purposes. There are a few exceptions, such as for communications about a drug or biologic that is currently being prescribed for the individual.

**5. What are an individual's rights regarding their PHI?**

Individuals have the right to:

*   Access and obtain a copy of their PHI.
*   Request an amendment to their PHI.
*   Request a restriction on the use or disclosure of their PHI.
*   Receive an accounting of disclosures of their PHI.

*Source: [HHS.gov - HIPAA for Professionals](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/index.html)*
